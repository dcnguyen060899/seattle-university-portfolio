Graduate Research Assistant — medical vision-language RAG Spring 2026 – Present
Seattle University | Advisor: Dr. Wenjing Yang
  • As a Graduate Research Assistant with Dr. Wenjing Yang, Duy runs a critical replication of a published retrieval-augmented mammography report-generation pipeline, which became a controlled experiment of his own design.
  • The position is ongoing, with weekly meetings.
  • The study runs on VinDr-Mammo — 5,000 studies from 5,000 patients, four views each, 20,000 images — with a 1,000-study held-out test split.
  • Duy designed and ran a 24-cell controlled experiment: four retrieval-encoder substrates, each evaluated frozen and fine-tuned, crossed with three frozen vision-language decoders.
  • The held-out majority-class retrieval floor is P@1 = 0.487; fine-tuning the mammography-native encoder produced the only arm of twenty-four to clear it, moving from 0.513 frozen to 0.585 fine-tuned.
  • Replacing the frozen generic retriever with the domain-native, contrastively fine-tuned encoder — decoder frozen throughout — raised suspicion macro-F1 from 0.35 to 0.48 on LLaVA-Med, a gain of 0.13.
  • The same substitution raised BI-RADS agreement by 0.10 on LLaVA-Med.
  • Isolating the fine-tuning step alone — holding the encoder family fixed — accounts for +0.11 on Qwen2.5-VL and +0.06 on LLaVA-Med.
  • Auditing his evaluation code against the paper he was replicating, Duy found that its published precision, recall and F1 are computed as weighted averages while its text describes them as macro-averaged — a metric-labelling artifact that changes how the reference numbers must be interpreted. His evaluator follows the paper's stated methodology, so every subsequent comparison runs inside his own pipeline rather than against the published numbers.
  • Evaluation is automated with reproducible, self-verifying generators in Python and pandas that recompute more than 100 reported metrics byte-exact from raw model predictions.
  • Duy wrote up the full analysis as a 37-page technical report for his advisor, Dr. Wenjing Yang.
  • The report was condensed into a first-author manuscript, "Separation Without Transfer: The Retrieval Encoder's Substrate, Not the VLM Decoder, Decides Whether Contrastive Fine-Tuning Transfers in Mammography Report Generation", submitted to the Pacific Symposium on Biocomputing 2027 with Dr. Wenjing Yang as co-author. It is under review.
  • The work is built in Python with PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, ChromaDB and scikit-learn, trained on a Google Colab A100.
  • VinDr-Mammo carries radiologist BI-RADS assessments, not biopsy-confirmed pathology, so every suspicion and BI-RADS result here is agreement with a radiologist's reading, not diagnostic accuracy.
  • The macro-F1 gain and the relative-accuracy gain are two renderings of the same measured effect, not two separate results.
  • The manuscript is under review; it is not accepted and not published.

Computational Neuroscience Lab, Seattle University | Advisor: Prof. Brian Fischer Full-time

  • The project is NIH CRCNS-funded research on how barn owls localize sound, and the data are neural recordings from that work.
  • Duy engineered an ETL pipeline in Python — pandas, SciPy and h5py — converting five or more proprietary electrophysiology formats (XDPHYS .iid, .itd and .bf, MATLAB .mat, headerless CSV) into a single normalized relational database.
  • The pipeline is end to end: it reads the raw lab files, applies the data-quality rules and loads a queryable database with no manual step in the middle.
  • Duy designed the relational schema and its ER model — documented in LaTeX — with foreign-key constraints and explicit data-quality rules covering missing-data handling, tracked exclusions and path remapping, consolidating scattered lab files into one queryable source of truth.
  • The shipped database is SQLite: seven normalized tables — owl, recording session, neuron, experiment, ILD condition tested, tuning parameters and spike reliability — plus five views built for the lab's own queries.
  • The database holds 195 neurons from 7 barn owls across 38 recording sessions, with 1,325 recording passes and 1,265 tuning-parameter rows.
  • Duy tuned the queries and the ETL to meet targets of under 200 milliseconds per query and under 30 seconds for a full load.
  • Lab students can now self-serve neuron-level queries that previously required manual file handling.
  • Duy ran SQL schema and data-integrity audits that caught and corrected problems — all-zero rows and weak-tuning exclusions — before they reached downstream analysis.
  • A second researcher's spike-reliability dataset is staged and schema-provisioned for load; its tables exist and hold no rows yet.
  • This work is ongoing, so its counts move; every figure here was verified against the lab database on 2 September 2026 and is re-derivable from it.
