For public-facing writing (portfolio essays, blog pages), two rules hold:

1. **No fabricated statistics, surveys, salary figures, company hiring numbers, or
   invented citations.** Labor-market claims stay at the level of mechanism. Economic
   concepts may be *named* (complementarity, comparative advantage, skill-biased
   technological change) but never attributed to a study.
2. **Do not import numerals from the PSB paper into public essays.** The design shape
   (four retrieval encoders, frozen vs fine-tuned, three frozen decoders) is fine; the
   metrics, floors, and deltas are not. Effect claims get carried in words with his own
   hedges intact ("nearly all of it landed on the routine cases").

**Why:** the essays argue from his experience and from economic logic. A single invented
number would undercut the paper's own honest-null-result register, which is the strongest
thing he has.

**How to apply:** register splits by artifact. The PSB manuscript is academic prose under
a numeric-invariance gate. The portfolio essay is warm, first person, direct, with varied
sentence length, one list at most, US spelling, few em-dashes. Do not let the paper voice
leak into the essay or vice versa. See [[user-career-thesis]].
